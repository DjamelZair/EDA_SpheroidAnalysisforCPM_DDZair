Theme 02·Measuring the shapes·radiomics reproducibility framework

Six numbers, eight models, one reliable answer

Two metrics, two stories. Only one of them is right for this pipeline.

Each cell cluster gets six shape numbers. The audit asks the only question that matters for the next step: do the AI-derived numbers agree with the human-derived numbers, well enough to trust them downstream? Using the radiomics-standard reproducibility framework (the published standard for medical imaging biomarkers), only one of eight models crosses the reliability bar, and only on two of the six numbers.

What this audit ultimately enables: each panel is one shape number, plotted over 5 days for three drug treatments. Every dot is automatically extracted from one microscopy frame by the AI segmenter. High-dose trametinib (cherry) collapses area; low-dose trametinib (gold) leaves the spheroid intact; PD098060 (teal) compacts rather than fragments. No human re-annotation is involved.

Two metrics, two stories

why agreement beats overlap
★ The metric that matters

Shape-number agreement

Take the predicted outline of a cell cluster, measure its area; take the human-annotated outline, measure its area; do those two areas agree across all 45 test images? Repeat for the other five shape numbers. This is what the simulation actually consumes, so this is what reliability needs to be measured against.

technical name: Lin's concordance score (CCC)

A model can have nearly-perfect pixel overlap and still consistently overestimate area by 30%. Pixel overlap won't catch that bias; agreement will.

The conventional metric

Pixel overlap

Take the predicted outline and the human outline, count how many pixels they share. This is the default metric in the AI segmentation literature, and it is the metric the deep-learning models are trained to optimise.

technical name: Dice coefficient

High pixel overlap does not guarantee that the shape numbers extracted from the mask match the shape numbers extracted from the human annotation. That mismatch is what this audit measures.

Same information as area but in linear units. Linear scaling makes it intuitive for plotting drug dose-response curves.

Captures asymmetry under stress. In practice this number is dominated by which fragments survived the segmentation step, so it is too noisy to trust here.

how "filled in" the outline is
Drops when the cluster fragments into pieces (gaps appear); rises when the cluster compacts into a tight ball. Different drug classes produce opposite signatures.

Length of the cluster's outer edge. Sensitive to small wiggles in the outline. Reliable from the chosen U-Net; not from any other model.
