Eight candidates, one winner

The conventional metric ranks them one way. The metric that matters for the downstream pipeline ranks them the opposite way.

Eight image-segmentation methods were compared on the same 45 microscopy images: a rule-based image-processing baseline, three nnU-Net variants, two SAM2 variants (Meta's foundation model fine-tuned on this dataset), a U-Net trained with heavy augmentation, and a pretrain-then-fine-tune approach. The winner was selected on the metric the next step in the pipeline cares about: do the AI-derived shape numbers agree with human-annotated ones? By that metric, the heavily-augmented U-Net wins by a wide margin even though three other models edge it out on pixel overlap.

What the chosen segmenter produces, on real microscopy frames the model has not seen at any point: cherry-red outline is the AI's call, panel boxes show the shape numbers extracted from each.

Shape-number agreement (concordance score)
Pixel overlap is mostly flat across the eight candidates (between 0.755 and 0.829) and ranks nnU-Net and SAM2 near the top. The shape-number agreement metric ranks the U-Net first by a wide margin and is the only one that reflects what the next step in the pipeline actually consumes. Yu et al. 2024 (J Nucl Med) · Carre et al. 2022 (Sci Rep) · Zwanenburg et al. 2020 (IBSI / Radiology)

Per-feature scoreboard

8 models × 6 shape numbers

Each cell is the agreement between AI-derived and human-derived value of one shape number, for one model. Cells outlined in gold cross the published reliability bar (0.85). Only the U-Net (heavy augmentation) crosses, on area and diameter. Roundness and elongation are unreliable for almost every model. Perimeter is reliable only for the U-Net.

Cleanup impact

overlap recovers · agreement does not
Pixel overlap with vs without cleanupa small post-processing step that removes tiny artefacts

A simple morphological cleanup step lifts the pixel-overlap score for nnU-Net by up to +0.044. The rule-based baseline barely benefits because its masks are already sparse. Important: on the metric that matters for the next stage, this cleanup does not move the needle. It rescues pixel overlap, not shape-number reliability.

Hardest test case

a heavily fragmented spheroid

When the spheroid disintegrates under high-dose drug, the segmentation problem fragments into many small pieces. Pixel overlap drops sharply for every model. Shape numbers behave differently: area and diameter are still reliable, while roundness and elongation collapse. This split is the entire reason this audit was needed.
