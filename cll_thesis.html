CLL spheroid pipeline

Turning microscopy of leukaemia cell clusters into a quantitative model of how they grow under drugs.

An end-to-end pipeline that takes brightfield microscopy of CLL (Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia) cell spheroids and produces validated parameters for a Cellular Potts Model simulation. Eight segmentation models were compared on the same 45-image test set using the radiomics reproducibility framework (the published standard for medical imaging biomarkers). The chosen model, a U-Net trained with heavy augmentation, is the only one whose extracted shape measurements pass the published reliability threshold for the features the downstream simulation actually consumes.

Glossary, jargon out

terms explained without the alphabet soup
Term

U-Net (heavy augmentation)

A standard neural-network architecture for image segmentation, shaped like a U. "Heavy augmentation" means we trained it with many randomly transformed copies of each image (random rotations, flips, brightness changes) because we only had 51 hand-annotated frames and needed to multiply the training signal.

Term

SAM2 (Meta foundation model)

A general-purpose segmentation model published by Meta, trained on 11 million natural images. We fine-tune its decoder on our microscopy data using bounding-box prompts that tell it roughly where the cluster is.

Term

nnU-Net

An auto-configured medical-imaging segmentation framework. Strong default for tumour and organ segmentation in clinical imaging. On this dataset it tends to merge adjacent fragments into smooth blobs, which inflates shape measurements.

Term

Concordance score

How well two values agree (the AI-derived shape number and the human-derived one). A pure correlation can be high while one is consistently double the other, so concordance penalises both rank disagreement and value bias. Threshold from radiomics: ≥ 0.85 means reliable enough to use.

Term

Cellular Potts Model

A physics-style cell-cluster simulation. Each cell is a small voxel patch; cells push and pull on each other according to adhesion energies and grow toward target volumes. Seven knobs (parameters) control its behaviour; matching simulated to real shape distributions recovers the knobs.

Term

Sobol sensitivity indices

A formal way to rank "which knob most controls a given output". Direct effect = the knob alone; total effect = the knob plus all interactions with the other knobs. A big gap between the two means that knob is entangled with others and cannot be identified independently.

How it works

Eight stages, plain English
Stage 01

Microscopy

Brightfield images of CLL cell spheroids in 96-well plates, captured every few hours by an IncuCyte machine across multiple drug treatments.

Stage 02

Image clean-up

Each image is contrast-normalised, the spheroid is roughly localised with a foundation model, and augmented copies are made for training.

Stage 03 · Headline

AI segmentation

Eight models compete to draw the outline. The winner is chosen on whether the resulting shape numbers are reliable, not just on pixel overlap with the human annotation.

Stage 04

Shape measurements

Six shape numbers per spheroid: area, equivalent diameter, perimeter, roundness, solidity, elongation.

Stage 05

Reliability audit

For each model and each shape number we check the agreement between AI and human against the published radiomics threshold. Most fail; the U-Net passes on size.

Stage 06

Cell-cluster simulation

A Cellular Potts Model: a physics-style simulation that grows cell clusters using simple energy rules (adhesion, perimeter, area). 7 knobs control its behaviour.

Stage 07

Sensitivity + matching

A global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices on a 1,152-run sample) ranks which simulation knobs matter. An optimal-transport step (next phase) matches simulated to real shape distributions.

Stage 08

Output

Estimated drug-specific simulation parameters, reproducible from raw image to numerical answer. One dashboard per stage.

The leaderboard

The two metrics tell opposite stories.

Pixel overlap (the conventional metric: how many pixels does the AI mask share with the human one) ranks nnU-Net and SAM2 near the top. Concordance score (the radiomics-standard metric: do the AI-derived shape numbers agree with the human-derived ones) ranks the U-Net first by a wide margin. The two metrics measure different things, and only the second one matters when the next step in the pipeline reads shape numbers, not masks.

When the downstream step consumes shape numbers, the segmenter has to be judged on shape-number reliability. Only the U-Net (heavy augmentation) crosses the published reliability bar, on area and diameter. Pixel-overlap rankings are misleading here.

Why this project

motivation in plain English

Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) is the most common adult leukaemia in Western countries. Patients respond differently to the same drug, and clinicians cannot yet predict which drug will work for which patient. To understand why, lab researchers grow 3D cell clusters (spheroids) from each patient's blood, treat them with candidate drugs, and film them under a microscope every few hours for 5 days.

Historically, that data is read off by hand: an expert measures the cluster size in a few frames and writes down a number. This project replaces that with an automated pipeline: AI segmentation, audited shape measurements, and a physics-style cell-cluster simulation that re-creates the same dynamics. The goal is to recover, from the microscopy alone, the simulation knob settings that explain a given drug's behaviour — turning a film into a quantitative drug-mechanism fingerprint.

The dataset shown on this site is one drug screen from the lab's Trametinib study. Trametinib is a clinically approved MEK inhibitor (used in melanoma; in trials for CLL); PD098060 is a research-grade MEK inhibitor we use as a comparison. The same pipeline applies to other drug screens in the lab (BTK inhibitors, BCL2 inhibitors); the audit framework on this site is the part that generalises.
