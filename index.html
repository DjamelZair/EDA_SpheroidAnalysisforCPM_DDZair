A real cell cluster from a CLL patient sample, with the cherry-red outline drawn by the AI segmenter. This is the input to the whole pipeline.

Why this project

CLL is the most common adult leukaemia in Western countries, and patients respond very differently to the same drug. To understand why, lab researchers grow 3D cell clusters from each patient's blood, treat them with candidate drugs, and film them under a microscope for several days.

The pipeline turns those films into a quantitative simulation of how the cells respond. The simulation parameters then become a fingerprint of the drug's mechanism.

Built end-to-end: image segmentation, reproducibility audit (radiomics standard), Cellular Potts simulation, global sensitivity, optimal-transport matching.

Theme 01
Drawing the outlines

Eight image-segmentation methods compared on the same test set: nnU-Net, SAM2 (Meta's foundation model with bounding-box prompts), classical image processing, U-Net variants. The winner is the U-Net trained with heavy augmentation. It wins on the metric that matters for the next step, not on conventional pixel overlap.

Theme 02
Measuring the shapes

Six shape numbers per cluster (area, diameter, roundness, solidity, perimeter, elongation). The audit asks the only question that matters: do the AI-derived numbers agree with human-annotated ones reliably enough to trust them? Two pass the published reliability bar; one is unusable; three are partial.

Theme 03 · Headline
Estimating the drug effect

A physics-style cell-cluster simulation (Cellular Potts Model) with seven mechanistic knobs: cell-cell adhesion, cell-medium adhesion, perimeter penalty, target volume, motility, and others. 1,152 simulation runs systematically sample the seven knobs at once. The next phase matches simulated and real shape distributions to recover the drug-specific knob settings.

04
Big-data classification · 2025

IMDB hit prediction.

Will a movie get a high audience score? A DuckDB + PySpark pipeline joins six IMDB tables, builds a 58 million-edge collaboration network between titles and crew, runs PageRank to produce centrality features, and trains a calibrated gradient-boosting classifier. Test AUC 0.965, validation/test gap 0.011. The tiny gap is a property of the design, not luck.

05
Representation learning · 2025

Unified tabular learning.

Can one neural network learn a shared representation across three completely different tabular datasets (particle physics, credit risk, forest cover)? A multi-head architecture trains on all three at once, producing a shared 64-dimensional latent space. The 2D projection on the left collapses that space to two dimensions; each colour is a class, and the clean separation across all three datasets is the empirical answer: yes.
